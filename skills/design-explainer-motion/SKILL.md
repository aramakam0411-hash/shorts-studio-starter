# Design an animated explanation

Start with the viewer's question, the useful change they should understand and
the actual available evidence. Keep the user's brand, audience, voice and language.
If a reference video is accessible, inspect real moving-picture samples before
describing its animation. A cover does not establish motion or the creator's tools.

Choose a recognizable subject and follow it through meaningful states: an input
becomes a result, an overview opens into a detail, or a claim meets its evidence.
Use pictures, objects, figures or actual product/source imagery where they explain
the idea. Give supporting elements consistent materials, depth and hierarchy.
Clear movement followed by a readable hold usually explains more than unrelated
drifting decorations. Vary treatments by story; a card layout is not a universal
template. A diagram can stay simple when that makes the point clearer.

Read [the tool selection guide](references/tool-selection.md) when choosing a
stack. Prefer tools already available in the user's environment. Check the actual
version, licence and output requirements before adding dependencies. A toolkit
does not provide ready-made artwork, permission to copy a creator or a better story.

For narrated work, use the supplied natural recording before committing timings.
Map the decisive actions to measured phrase cues, preserving meaningful pauses.
Do not stretch speech to fit a template or call guessed word times aligned.
If sound is not available, return an untimed storyboard or an explicitly silent
study when that serves the request. Music and source assets need suitable rights.

Deliver a concise editable brief: viewer payoff, chosen subject, meaningful state
changes, assets and their provenance, typography/colour/material choices, selected
tool with a reason, and any supplied cue times. Include an opening, the densest
transition and the settled result to inspect at phone size. Name missing inputs
that genuinely prevent a truthful demonstration. No growth guarantee or automatic
publishing follows from a visual plan.
