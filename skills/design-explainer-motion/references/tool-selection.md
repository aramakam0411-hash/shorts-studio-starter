# Choose the smallest suitable motion stack

Official repositories and licences checked November15,2026. Recheck the actual
revision before installing or redistributing a dependency. These are options,
not evidence that a particular creator used them.

| Job | Tool | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layered UI, papers, shapes and transitions | HTML/CSS/SVG plus a browser | Direct control of layout and materials; absolute-time frame functions can be rendered deterministically |
| Code-driven 2D explanations with an editor | [Motion Canvas](https://github.com/motion-canvas/motion-canvas) | A dedicated scene/timeline workflow for authored explanations |
| Timelines for DOM, SVG or an existing 3D scene | [Anime.js](https://github.com/juliangarnier/anime) | Coordinate state changes and seek a paused timeline to an explicit time |
| A real spatial object, lighting or camera | [Three.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js) | Original 3D geometry and scene rendering, when depth or viewpoint explains something |
| Capture rendered browser pictures | [Playwright](https://playwright.dev/python/docs/screenshots) | Local browser screenshots; the agent still authors and reviews the scene |

Motion Canvas, Anime.js and Three.js publish MIT licences. Keep the applicable
notice when redistributing their code; examples, models, fonts and other assets
may have different rights. Browser screenshots of someone else's work do not
become yours merely because the renderer is open source.

For the included layered study, HTML/CSS/SVG is sufficient. The separate
render-layered-motion skill contains a portable browser-to-MP4 helper. For a
different renderer, keep its native timeline and actual export evidence rather
than forcing an HTML wrapper. Do not install a new runtime merely to imitate a
look that the current tools can already produce.

Keep one useful subject recognizable through a scene/detail/result change.
Move and settle on the relevant speech cue. Inspect actual encoded transitions,
not only attractive stills. Report actual access/inspection limits and avoid
claiming human hearing, lip-sync quality or retention from technical checks.
